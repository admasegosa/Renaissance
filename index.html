We developed new Artificial Intelligence methods to make urban mobility and urban freight transport more efficient, sustainable, safe and resilient, and validated them with real data from European cities.

What RENAISSANCE achieved

Transport is central to the European and Spanish economy, but it also brings congestion, emissions and road crashes. RENAISSANCE set out to tackle these problems with new Artificial Intelligence techniques that exploit today's abundance of mobility data and the arrival of electric, shared and on-demand services.

Over 36 months the project met all its objectives and milestones. Its methods were integrated into a software toolkit and tested on real use cases in several European cities. The work has led to new European, national and regional projects and to a technology transfer contract with industry.

Three research lines, one toolkit

Each line combined optimisation and machine learning, and was validated on data from real cities. The data work of work package 2 (more than 50 sources identified and harmonised) fed all three.

AWork package 3

Collaborative and multimodal last-mile logistics

More realistic vehicle routing models that include electric fleets, different transport modes and time windows, and new algorithms to solve them. The goal: fewer kilometres and emissions per delivery and more use of clean vehicles.

  1. Hyper-heuristics and reinforcement learningAdaptive simulated annealing with reinforcement learning for the electric vehicle routing problem, and a reusable reinforcement learning environment for routing.
  2. Ant colony and hybrid metaheuristicsA multi-strategy ant colony system for routing with time windows, and methods for dynamic location and warehouse layout problems.
  3. An intelligent last-mile solverUsed to simulate urban logistics scenarios (consolidation centres, low-emission zones, pedestrian areas, micro-hubs) in two European cities.
BWork package 4

Transport network management

Optimisation methods to synchronise on-demand services with public transport, tune traffic signals and design dynamic congestion pricing, tested with the Aimsun Next and SUMO simulators.

  1. On-demand transport and freight on transitAdaptive Large Neighbourhood Search to synchronise demand-responsive transport with transit, and simulation-based optimisation of parcels carried on autonomous on-demand services in a peri-urban area.
  2. Signals, pricing, roadworks and superblocksLLM-guided Bayesian optimisation of traffic signals, dynamic congestion pricing, roadworks scheduling, and the evaluation of superblocks.
  3. Faster evaluation with surrogatesGSPINN, a physics-informed graph surrogate that predicts trip travel times in milliseconds, and a study of neural surrogates inside Differential Evolution.
CWork package 5

Road safety for vulnerable road users

Crash prediction models based on graphs and deep learning that integrate new and heterogeneous data sources, and work on making those models explainable and trustworthy for road authorities.

  1. Graph data models of the road networkA labelled graph framework that brings together crash records, traffic density and connected vehicle data to find accident hotspots in a large city.
  2. Explainable predictionsGraphLIME and a study of how preprocessing, hyperparameter tuning and algorithm choice change what crash models explain.
  3. Better calibrated AutoMLThe role of ensemble diversity in the accuracy and calibration of AutoML, as a basis for more robust risk prediction.

Software toolkit. The three lines meet in a modular toolkit (work package 6) with modules for logistics optimisation, network optimisation and crash analysis. It was tested and validated on the use cases below.

Use cases

The methods were validated with real data from European and international cities, provided by public open-data portals and by partners under confidentiality agreements. To respect those agreements, the use cases are described here in general terms.

City-centre parcel deliveryLogistics

Parcel deliveries of a postal operator in the centre of a European capital: consolidation, low-emission zones, pedestrian areas and micro-hubs, across four demand levels.

Historic centre with a zero-emission zoneLogistics

Postal deliveries in the historic centre of a Spanish city with a zero-emission zone, an urban consolidation centre and different levels of fleet electrification.

Peri-urban on-demand transportNetwork

Demand-responsive transport integrated with public transport, and parcels carried on autonomous on-demand services in a peri-urban area.

Congestion pricingNetwork

Dynamic congestion pricing strategies evaluated on a mesoscopic traffic model of a European capital.

Traffic signal controlNetwork

Optimisation of traffic signal plans on a mesoscopic traffic model of a metropolitan network.

Roadworks planningNetwork

Scheduling of roadworks in a regional multimodal network to reduce congestion and emissions.

Accident hotspotsRoad safety

Accident hotspot prediction for vulnerable road users in a large city, from open crash records, road network, traffic demand and connected vehicle events.

SuperblocksNetwork

Optimal location of superblocks in a city network with agent-based and microscopic traffic simulation.

BenchmarksMethods

Public benchmarks for routing, AutoML, image segmentation and traffic simulation, so that results can be reproduced and compared.

Open science and data

RENAISSANCE publishes its results in open access and manages its research data following the FAIR principles and its Data Management Plan. Data are as open as possible and as closed as necessary: what comes from confidential sources stays protected.

All open outputs are in one place: the RENAISSANCE community on Zenodo

Publications and open datasets are deposited in Zenodo, linked to grant PID2022-140612OB-I00 of the Spanish State Research Agency. Every record has its own DOI, version history and licence. Community: renaissance-pid2022-140612ob-i00

Data Management Plan

  • Responsible for the plan and data access: Antonio D. Masegosa (principal investigator).
  • Data steward: Asier Moreno Emborujo (lead of data collection and harmonisation).
  • Formats: open formats such as CSV, JSON, GeoJSON, GTFS and SUMO XML, with a README describing variables and units.
  • Personal data: none. Confidential data arrived anonymised or aggregated.
  • Preservation: open outputs are kept indefinitely on Zenodo. Third-party data are deleted as their agreements require.

Questions about the data or requests to reuse them: write to ad.masegosa@deusto.es.
